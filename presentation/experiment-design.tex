\section{Research Question}

\emph{Do different instruction types (format, constraint, persona, safety, tool use) exhibit measurably different decay rates in multi-turn LLM conversations? If so, can a Bayesian tracker exploit those differences to maintain equivalent compliance at lower token cost than uniform reinforcement?}

\section{Three-Phase Design}

\subsection{Phase 1 --- Measure Decay Curves (Baseline)}

\begin{tabular}{@{} l l @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Model & Claude Sonnet 4.6 \\
Conversations & 20+ (4 pools $\times$ 5 convs) \\
Turns per conversation & 30 \\
Reinforcement & None \\
Measurement & 0--3 graded compliance per type per turn \\
Output & Forgetting curves $S(t) = S_0 \cdot \gamma^t$ per type \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.5em}
All 5 instruction types active simultaneously. Every 5th turn includes a \emph{temptation} designed to probe a specific instruction type. 4 randomized user message pools control for topic effects.

\subsection{Phase 2 --- Test the Intervention}

\begin{tabular}{@{} l l l @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Token budget} \\
\midrule
Control & No reinforcement & 0 \\
Uniform & Re-inject all 5 instructions every 5 turns & $\sim$250 tokens \\
Selective & Re-inject lowest-$P(C_i)$ instructions every 5 turns & $\sim$250 tokens \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.5em}
Same model, prompts, and pools as Phase 1. 20 turns $\times$ 20 conversations per condition. Bayesian tracker uses decay rates $\gamma_i$ fit from Phase 1 data.

\subsection{Phase 3 --- Multi-Model Validation}

Decay curves (no intervention) on 4 additional architectures: Qwen 3.5-27B, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite, GPT-5.4-nano, MiniMax M2.5. 5 conversations $\times$ 20 turns per model.

\section{Compliance Scoring (Graded Response Model)}

\begin{tabular}{@{} c l @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Score} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
3 & Flawless --- instruction followed completely \\
2 & Good --- minor deviations \\
1 & Vague --- partial compliance, drift visible \\
0 & Failed --- instruction violated or ignored \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.5em}
Bayesian tracker maintains a Gaussian belief $\theta_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2)$ per instruction. Updated via GRM likelihood with Laplace approximation. Between turns: $\mu_i \leftarrow \gamma_i \cdot \mu_i$, $\sigma_i$ increases. Reinforcement targets instructions with lowest $P(\text{score} \geq 2 \mid \theta_i)$.

\section{Metrics}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{AUCC} (Area Under the Compliance Curve) --- integral of mean compliance over turns. Higher = better retention.
\item \textbf{Token efficiency} --- AUCC per token spent on reinforcement. The punchline: does selective achieve similar AUCC to uniform at fewer tokens?
\item \textbf{Decay rate $\gamma_i$} --- per-instruction-type. Core hypothesis: $\gamma_{\text{safety}} < \gamma_{\text{format}}$.
\end{enumerate}
