RUBRIC — Peer review of a research paper for 55 JAIIO / ASAID
(Argentine Symposium on Artificial Intelligence — AI-safety & LLM-evaluation paper)

Grounded in the public reviewer forms of NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL Rolling
Review (ARR) and AAAI; the NeurIPS Paper Checklist and the ML Reproducibility
Checklist (Pineau 2020); AAC&U VALUE rubrics (Written Communication; Inquiry &
Analysis); APA JARS-Quant reporting standards; AI-safety venue criteria
(SaTML, AAAI-SRRAI, SoLaR @ NeurIPS); LLM-evaluation rigor guidance
(contamination, prompt-sensitivity, LLM-as-judge reporting); and the 55 JAIIO
"tipos de trabajo" rules. Sources listed at the end.

==============================================================================
CONTEXT FOR THE GRADER
==============================================================================
A full research article submitted to JAIIO/ASAID: up to 14 pages, empirical +
Bayesian methodology, relevant to AI safety, studying how language models retain
user-stated instructions over long conversations. English paper with a Spanish
title/abstract/keywords (venue requirement). Review like a venue PC member, not
a copy editor.

Following ARR, keep two axes ORTHOGONAL: SOUNDNESS (is it correct/rigorous?) is
separate from CONTRIBUTION/EXCITEMENT (does it matter?). A paper can be sound but
unexciting, or exciting but unsound. Score each criterion, then derive the
overall recommendation; give your CONFIDENCE separately.

For EACH criterion: assign a level (Excellent / Good / Adequate / Weak), write
1-3 sentences of specific feedback citing the section, and assign points.
Reward calibrated, evidence-backed claims; penalize overclaiming, decorative
framing, and unsupported assertions.

Level anchors (general; criterion-specific anchors below):
  Excellent — exemplary; among the best of its type; nothing a reviewer flags.
  Good      — solid; minor gaps that do not affect the conclusions.
  Adequate  — acceptable but with real weaknesses a reviewer would raise.
  Weak      — a substantive problem that undermines the contribution.

==============================================================================
SCORED CRITERIA (weights sum to 100)
==============================================================================

1. SOUNDNESS & METHODOLOGY — 25  (load-bearing; the universal review axis)
   Validity of the study design and of the statistical model; validity of the
   measurement instruments; whether artifacts/confounds are ruled out.
   Sub-checks (LLM-evaluation rigor):
     - Statistics as a gate: estimates carry uncertainty (intervals/error bars);
       the variability source is stated (seeds/splits/decoding) and the method
       named; multiple runs/epochs where stochastic. [NeurIPS checklist 7; Pineau]
     - Model appropriateness: likelihood matched to the data (ordinal treated as
       ordinal, not binarized/averaged); hierarchy/pooling justified; convergence
       (R-hat, ESS, divergences), posterior predictive checks, model comparison
       (e.g., LOO), and prior sensitivity reported.
     - Contamination / leakage addressed where benchmarks are reused.
     - Prompt-sensitivity and decoding robustness acknowledged (non-determinism).
     - LLM-as-judge done right: bias-adjusted (not raw judge scores), with
       judge model+version, agreement statistics (chance-corrected kappa/alpha),
       and calibration; OR judge-free scoring justified.
   Excellent: design isolates the effect; the model is justified and fully
     checked; plausible artifacts are tested and ruled out; eval numbers are
     statistically and contamination-robust.
   Adequate: mostly sound but a check is missing (e.g., no model comparison, or
     error bars without a stated source).
   Weak: design confounds the effect, model unjustified/unchecked, or a likely
     artifact is left unaddressed.

2. CONTRIBUTION / SIGNIFICANCE / NOVELTY — 18
   Is the contribution new and does it matter to the field? (ARR "Excitement";
   NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR "Contribution"; AAAI "Significance of the Problem".)
   Excellent: a clearly novel question/method/finding, explicitly differentiated
     from prior work, that the community will build on.
   Weak: incremental or already covered by cited work; low impact.

3. CLAIMS–EVIDENCE CALIBRATION — 12
   Does every claim carry its evidence and uncertainty? Established vs preliminary
   separated. No claim stronger than the design supports (levels vs rates;
   snapshot vs decay; correlation vs cause). Abstract/intro match the body.
   [NeurIPS checklist 1: "claims match contributions and scope".]
   Excellent: claims precisely calibrated; hedging proportionate; no abstract-body
     mismatch.
   Weak: overclaiming, bare point estimates, or abstract contradicts results.

4. CLARITY & PRESENTATION — 10
   Structure, prose, figures/tables. Plain, declarative, argumentative writing;
   one claim per paragraph; figures legible and referenced; cross-refs resolve.
   (NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR 4-point "Presentation"; AAC&U Written Communication.)
   Excellent: the argument is followable end-to-end on first read; figures earn
     their space.
   Weak: confusing structure, garbled sentences, or uninformative figures.

5. REPRODUCIBILITY — 12  (first-class concern; itemized per Pineau/NeurIPS)
   Are data, code, models, prompts, temperature/token caps, seeds, exclusions,
   eval-harness version, and scoring stated enough to reproduce? Hyperparameter
   ranges + selection method? Compute reported? [ARR Reproducibility 1-5;
   NeurIPS checklist 4-8; Pineau; model cards/datasheets where relevant.]
   Excellent: a competent reader could reproduce the main results; choices and
     exclusions are explicit; artifacts/versions disclosed.
   Weak: key parameters, data, prompts, or code undisclosed.

6. RELATED WORK & POSITIONING — 6
   Pertinent, synthesized (not a list of isolated summaries); explains why each
   cited work matters and where this paper sits relative to the closest work.
   Excellent: sharp positioning against the nearest prior art.
   Weak: missing key work, or a summary list with no synthesis.

7. LIMITATIONS & HONESTY — 7  (required reflection at NeurIPS/ICML/ARR)
   Are limitations exhaustive and self-critical? Proxy measures admitted as
   proxies? Negative/ambiguous results reported honestly?
   Excellent: candid, complete; the paper names its own weakest points.
   Weak: limitations token, missing, or contradicted elsewhere.

8. AI-SAFETY FRAMING / THREAT MODEL — 6  (venue-specific: ASAID)
   Is the safety relevance a precise threat model (what fails, for whom, under
   what conditions — e.g., unsupervised deployed agents, silent guardrail decay),
   cited, and NOT overclaimed beyond what was tested? [SaTML; AAAI-SRRAI; SoLaR.]
   Excellent: a concrete threat model that follows from the results without
     claiming safety experiments that were not run.
   Weak: safety framing decorative, vague, or overclaimed.

9. ETHICS / BROADER IMPACT / RESPONSIBLE RELEASE — 4
   Societal impact discussed; dual-use/misuse considered; responsible release of
   any high-misuse-risk artifacts; human-subjects/data handled properly.
   [NeurIPS Code of Ethics + checklist 9-15; ARR Limitations & Societal Impact.]
   Excellent: impacts and any release risks addressed proportionately.
   Weak: relevant impact/misuse considerations ignored.

==============================================================================
FORMAT & COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST  (pass/fail gate — list any failure; do NOT award
"Strong accept" if any item fails). Per 55 JAIIO "tipos de trabajo".
==============================================================================
[ ] Full article, self-contained, within the 14-page limit.
[ ] Format per venue: 55 JAIIO generic rule = A4, Times New Roman 10pt,
    single-spaced; the ASAID/LNCS template is the alternative the symposium
    uses — confirm which applies and that the paper is internally consistent.
[ ] English title + abstract + keywords AND Spanish título + resumen
    (≈150-250 words) + palabras clave.
[ ] References in APA-7; every in-text citation has an entry and vice versa
    (no orphans).
[ ] ORCID present for the author(s).
[ ] Use-of-LLMs disclosed; no LLM-generated text beyond experimental material
    or language/readability improvement (JAIIO policy); author takes
    responsibility.
[ ] Review stage: anonymized (ASAI is double-blind) OR camera-ready
    de-anonymized (author, affiliation, ORCID, email) — per the current stage.
[ ] Figures/tables referenced in text; no unresolved refs; clean layout
    (no overfull/garbled text).

==============================================================================
SCORING & OUTPUT
==============================================================================
Weighted total /100 = sum of the nine criterion points.
Recommendation mapping (NeurIPS/ICML-style accept bands):
  85-100  Strong accept
  70-84   Accept
  60-69   Borderline accept (accept with required revisions)
  50-59   Borderline reject (major revision)
  < 50    Reject
Also give a CONFIDENCE self-rating (1-5): 5 certain · 4 confident · 3 fairly
confident · 2 willing to defend but may have missed details · 1 educated guess.

Report:
  - total /100 and the recommendation band,
  - per-criterion: level + points + 1-3 sentences of actionable feedback,
  - the format/compliance checklist result (list failures),
  - 3 biggest strengths,
  - the 3 highest-priority fixes,
  - your confidence (1-5).
